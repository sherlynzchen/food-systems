I. Overview — The Global Food System

The world grows enough
to feed everyone.
So why doesn't it?

The primary goal of any food system is simple: feed people affordably and reliably, while delivering incomes for those who work in it. Global output has outpaced population growth since the 1960s — yet the system still fails hundreds of millions. Follow the flow from field to fate.

Total production
9,800T
Trillion kcal produced globally per year — enough for 10 billion people at 2,500 kcal/day.
Lost or wasted
~30%
Of all food produced is lost post-harvest, in transit, at retail, or in homes. The largest single addressable intervention.
Used as animal feed
31%
Of global crop calories are fed to livestock, returning only 15–20% as meat and dairy — a caloric inefficiency at scale.

I. Overview — Supply vs Requirement

Global supply of calories,
protein and fat has
outpaced requirements.

Since 1961, global food supply has grown at +2.1–2.6% per year across all three macronutrients — consistently outpacing the ~1.6% annual population growth rate. The gap between supply and nutritional requirement has widened every decade.

I. Overview — Land & Labour Productivity

Every breakthrough
carried a hidden invoice.

Output growth has been driven by both land and labour productivity. Agricultural land use has levelled off, while the global agricultural workforce has fallen 15% since peaking in 2002 — yet productivity gains have come with significant ecological costs. Hover any branch to read the trade-off.

III. Drivers — Affordability & Agricultural Labour

For the poorest,
food is not a choice.
It is the choice.

Food insecurity is highest in countries with low agricultural labour productivity. Because actual food expenditure varies far less than income, low-productivity countries spend a far greater share of earnings on food — leaving little margin for income volatility.
